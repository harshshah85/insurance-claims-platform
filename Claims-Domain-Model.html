Claims Domain Model

The language of a claim, modeled honestly — bounded contexts, aggregates, and the lifecycle a claim travels from first notice to final payment. Get the words right and the rest of the platform falls into place.

Prepared by Harsh Shah  |  June 2026
The model is Domain-Driven Design over an event-sourced core. It works out to 9 bounded contexts, 11 aggregates, and a 36-event domain catalog (the catalog itself lives in the Event Streaming Architecture doc). Life (the death claim) and DI (income replacement) run on the same platform but follow different lifecycle state machines, both laid out below.
How the agents map to the model: the platform is agent-assisted by design. Each agent worker is a first-class collaborator scoped to a context it serves, drafting recommendations a human acts on — so the agents operate the existing nine contexts faster rather than forming a tenth. Authority is unchanged: every authoritative decision still belongs to its aggregate (a denial on the Decision aggregate must carry a reason code and cite the provision), enforced against a human's decision. Agents reason, humans decide. (See AI-Assisted & Agentic Decisioning.)

1 Bounded Contexts

Nine contexts, each owning its events, its aggregates, and its team. Contexts integrate only through published events — never shared tables.

1 · Intake & Claim Filing

Captures First Notice of Loss from every channel (client portal, advisor desktop, service-rep phone, secure message, mail/upload, IVR). Owns the channel-agnostic intake contract and the initial claim creation.

2 · Policy & Coverage

Resolves the claim to a policy via Policy Admin CDC. Confirms in-force status, coverage type, face amount / benefit amount, and contestability window. Quarantines unresolved policy numbers.

3 · Party & Identity

Models claimant, insured (decedent for Life), beneficiary, advisor, and servicing rep. Owns identity verification and beneficiary designation resolution.

4 · Requirements

Tracks outstanding requirements (death certificate, proof of loss, attending-physician statement, claimant statement, beneficiary forms). Raises, satisfies, waives, and ages requirements.

5 · Adjudication & Decisioning

Computes the STP eligibility score, routes to the low-touch or manual path, and records the approve / deny / pend decision with rationale and the responsible authority.

6 · Fraud & Investigations

Scores fraud risk, refers high-risk claims to the Special Investigations Unit, and tracks investigation outcome (cleared / substantiated). Blocks the low-touch path on referral.

7 · Disability Management

DI-only. Manages recurring benefit periods, periodic recertification, return-to-work, benefit offsets (e.g. Social Security, other coverage), and benefit termination.

8 · Payments & Money Movement

Owns the disbursement saga: schedule, hold, dual-control approval, OFAC, tax withholding, settlement, and reconciliation. (Detailed in Correspondence & Money Movement.)

9 · Correspondence & Compliance

Drives inbound/outbound correspondence and enforces regulatory timers (acknowledge, decide, pay) and the immutable audit record. (Detailed in its own docs.)

3 How Life and DI Claims Differ

Same platform, two very different jobs. The biggest difference is who starts the claim — and that one fact drives nearly everything else.

A life (death) claim is beneficiary-driven: the insured has died, so someone else — a spouse, a child, the estate — files it. That person usually isn't in our systems and may not even know the policy number. A DI claim is client-initiated: the living policyholder files their own claim because they can't work, and they're already a known, logged-in customer. Most of the differences below fall out of that one fact.

 Life — death claim (beneficiary-driven)Disability Income — DI (client-initiated)
Who files itA beneficiary or the estate — often a stranger to us, grieving, may not know policy detailsThe policyholder themselves — an existing, authenticated customer
What we're really doingVerify the death and who's entitled, then pay a lump sum onceConfirm they can't work, then pay a monthly benefit and keep checking it stays correct
The hard part to buildThe front door: proving the identity of an unknown filer, resolving who the beneficiary is, verifying the death, and finding the policy from the decedent's name/SSNThe long tail: recurring payments, periodic re-checks, return-to-work, and reducing the benefit for other income (e.g. Social Security) over months or years
ShapeOne-time — pays once and closesLong-running — an open case that pays each period until recovery or the benefit ends
Defining gateContestability — within 2 years of issue we may review the original application before payingRecertification — each period we re-confirm they're still eligible before the next payment
Where fraud shows upMisrepresentation on the application, a faked death, the wrong beneficiaryDisability claimed after recovery, unreported income, missed offsets
Business caseLow volume, large payout, one shot. The win is speed and getting it right in a hard moment — a brand and advisor-retention moment for the familyHigher touch, runs for years. The win is stopping leakage — catching return-to-work, applying offsets, not overpaying month after month
Mostly handled inOnboarding (intake identity + quarantine recovery), Decisioning (the contestability gate)Money Movement (the recurring payment saga + offsets) and the Disability Management context below
One nuance: "life claim" here means the death claim — the common, beneficiary-driven case. Living/accelerated benefits on a life policy are filed by the insured themselves, so they behave more like the client-initiated side. We treat the death claim as the primary life flow.

The two lifecycles

A life claim is mostly a straight line that ends in one payment. Contestability is the branch that can pull it out of the fast path.

NOTIFIED └─► INTAKE (FnolCaptured, PolicyResolved | PolicyUnresolved→QUARANTINE) └─► DOCUMENTS_PENDING (RequirementRaised: death certificate, proof, beneficiary forms) └─► IN_REVIEW (requirements satisfied, StpEligibilityScored, FraudScored) ├─► CONTESTABILITY_REVIEW (if within 2-yr window → manual underwriting review) ├─► SIU_HOLD (if fraud risk ≥ 60) └─► DECISION ├─► APPROVED ─► PAYMENT_SCHEDULED ─► PAID ─► CLOSED ├─► DENIED ─► CLOSED (reason code + provision cited) └─► PENDED ─► back to DOCUMENTS_PENDING / IN_REVIEW side states: WITHDRAWN, REOPENED (e.g. additional beneficiary), QUARANTINE

A DI claim opens and then loops — paying and re-checking each period until the person returns to work or the benefit ends. That re-check loop (the RecertificationDue timer) is the heartbeat of an active DI claim.

NOTIFIED ─► INTAKE ─► ELIGIBILITY_REVIEW (elimination period, occupation definition, APS) └─► DECISION ├─► APPROVED ─► BENEFIT_ACTIVE (BenefitPeriodOpened; recurring disbursement) │ │ │ ├─◄─► RECERTIFICATION (RecertificationDue → Received; loops each period) │ ├───► OFFSET_APPLIED (SSDI / other coverage reduces benefit) │ ├───► SUSPENDED (recert overdue / RTW pending verification) │ └───► RETURN_TO_WORK | BENEFIT_EXHAUSTED ─► CLOSED └─► DENIED ─► CLOSED
