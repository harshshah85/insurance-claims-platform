Glossary

The insurance, event-driven, and AI terms used across these documents — defined once, in plain language.

Prepared by Harsh Shah  |  June 2026
This platform borrows from three vocabularies — life and disability claims, event-driven systems, and an LLM assist layer. This page defines the shorthand each doc leans on, so you can read any one of them on its own.

1 Claims & Insurance

TermMeaning
FNOL — First Notice of LossThe moment a claim is first filed — a death reported, a disability claimed. The "front door" of the platform.
Life (death) claimFiled by a beneficiary after the insured dies. One-time, large payout; the hard part is the front door (identity-proof an unknown filer, verify the death, find the policy).
DI — Disability IncomeIncome-replacement claim filed by the policyholder. The hard part is the long tail — recurring payments, recertification, return-to-work, offsets.
STP — Straight-Through ProcessingA clean, low-risk claim decided automatically with no human touch — what we call the auto-ticket / low-touch path.
AdjudicationDeciding a claim — approve, deny, or pend pending more information.
SIU — Special Investigations UnitThe fraud-investigation team. High-risk claims are referred here and have payment held.
NIGO — Not In Good OrderA claim missing required documents or information; it can't proceed until resolved.
IDV / KYCIdentity Verification / Know Your Customer — proving the claimant and payee are who they say they are.
ContestabilityAn early window (typically the first two policy years) in which the insurer may contest a claim for misrepresentation. Contestable claims always get human review.
DMF — Death Master FileAn external death-records source used to verify a reported death.
UCSPA / prompt-payUnfair Claims Settlement Practices Act — state rules that require claims be handled and paid within set timeframes, with interest owed for late payment.
RecertificationPeriodic proof, on a DI claim, that the claimant is still disabled and entitled to continued benefits.
RTW / OffsetReturn To Work; and an offset reduces a DI benefit by other income (e.g. social-insurance payments).
1099-RThe U.S. tax form for reporting certain claim/retirement distributions; relevant to tax withholding on payouts.

2 Event-Driven Architecture

TermMeaning
Event sourcingStoring a thing as the ordered list of events that happened to it, rather than a row you overwrite. The log is the system of record.
CQRSCommand Query Responsibility Segregation — the write side (append events) is separate from the read side (query simple projections built from those events).
Read model / projectionA query-friendly view (a table, a search index) built by replaying events. Disposable — drop it and rebuild from the log.
AggregateA consistency boundary — a cluster of data (e.g. the Claim) that enforces its own rules and is updated as a unit.
Bounded contextA self-contained slice of the domain with its own model and language (Intake, Decisioning, Payments…). This platform has 9.
Topic / partition / keyKafka's units: events live on a topic, split across partitions; the key (e.g. claim_id) decides the partition and guarantees per-key ordering.
SagaA long-running process done as small, individually reversible steps with compensations — used for money movement instead of one big distributed transaction.
Transactional outboxA pattern that writes a row and its event in one database transaction, then publishes the event separately — so you never "commit the row but lose the event."
CDC — Change Data CaptureStreaming another system's changes (here, the policy admin system) onto a topic as they happen, instead of batch ETL.
EOS — Exactly-Once SemanticsA processing guarantee that a consume→transform→produce cycle happens once and only once, even on retry.
Compaction vs retentionTwo cleanup policies: retention keeps the full history for a time window (used for the event log); compaction keeps only the latest value per key (used for latest-state topics). Compacting a log would discard history.
Avro / Schema RegistryA compact binary message format (Avro) plus a registry that enforces compatible schema evolution, so producers and consumers don't break each other.
DLQ — Dead-Letter QueueWhere messages land after exhausting retries, with failure metadata, for an operator to inspect — never blindly auto-replayed.
Correlation / Causation IDTrace fields on every event: correlation stays constant for a whole claim journey; causation points at the immediate predecessor event.

3 AI & Decisioning

TermMeaning
LLM — Large Language ModelThe language model the agent workers call to read documents and draft reasoning. Vendor-neutral and swappable; it sees tokenized data only.
Agent / agenticAn LLM-driven worker that plans and acts over multiple steps (read a document, reconcile facts, draft a packet) rather than answering a single prompt.
Skill vs toolA tool is a concrete callable action (look up a policy, fetch a document); a skill is a packaged procedure the agent follows to use tools toward a goal.
HITL — Human-in-the-LoopA person reviews and approves (or overrides) what the AI proposes. On this platform a human holds decision authority on every AI-touched claim.
Grounding / citationTying every AI claim back to a specific source in the record, so a recommendation is checkable rather than asserted.
AbstentionA first-class outcome where the AI declines to recommend on a low-confidence or conflicting claim and escalates it to manual review.
claims.ai.*The derived advisory stream the agents write to — separate from the system-of-record topics, the way read models are derived from the log. Never auto-decides or moves money.
ADR — Architecture Decision RecordA short, dated record of a significant design decision: the context, the options weighed, the choice, and the consequence.
