Claims Decisioning & Fraud

How a claim earns its way to an instant, no-touch payment — or gets gently set aside for a person to look closer. STP eligibility, the low-touch path, and fraud routing, designed so the honest majority never pay for the rare bad actor.

Prepared by Harsh Shah  |  June 2026
Every claim gets two scores. An STP score (how clean and safe is this to automate?) and a fraud score (how suspicious is it?). The auto-ticket path only opens when the first is high and the second is low. Fraud is a hard gate: a suspicious claim never auto-pays, however clean it looks otherwise.

1 STP Score — Can We Auto-Ticket It?

Decides whether a claim can run straight through with no human (auto-ticketed) or needs a human to look at it. "STP" = straight-through processing.

STP = (policy_in_force × 0.25)
     + (coverage_clear × 0.20)
     + (doc_completeness × 0.20)
     + (identity_verified × 0.15)
     + (fraud_inverse × 0.10)
     + (amount_in_band × 0.10)
FactorWeight0 (worst) → 100 (best)
policy_in_force0.250 if lapsed / unresolved; 100 if confirmed in-force on date of loss with premiums current
coverage_clear0.20Coverage type clearly covers the claimed event; no riders/exclusions in dispute; not in contestability window
doc_completeness0.20% of required documents received and validated (death certificate, proof, beneficiary forms / APS for DI)
identity_verified0.15Claimant & payee identity verification pass; beneficiary designation unambiguous (shares sum to 100%)
fraud_inverse0.10100 − fraud risk score (couples the two models so a clean-looking but risky claim can't auto-pass)
amount_in_band0.10100 if face/benefit ≤ the auto-approval limit; decays above it (large amounts always get a human)
These weights are a starting point, not a fitted result. The two-decimal weights here (and the fraud weights below) and the 85/65/40 tier cut-offs are informed heuristics set with adjudicators — there is no labeled outcome history to fit them to at launch, and presenting them as anything more would overclaim. They are chosen to be robust: the decision is insensitive to small weight changes near the 85 boundary, because the hard guardrails — not the soft weights — do the decisive gating. As labeled outcomes accumulate, the feedback flywheel recalibrates both the weights and the thresholds against the false-auto rate and the fraud-unit hit rate tracked in §6.

Decision tiers

STP ScoreTierPath
≥ 85AUTOLow-touch / auto-ticket. System adjudicates, emits ClaimAutoApproved, schedules payment, and triggers correspondence — no queue, no human.
65 – 84ASSISTEDAuto-prepared decision packet routed to a service rep for a one-click confirm; system did the work, human signs off.
40 – 64STANDARDFull manual adjudication in the worklist; system supplies score breakdown and facts but makes no decision.
< 40COMPLEX / fraud unitComplex claim — large amount, contestability, or fraud signal. Senior adjudicator and/or fraud unit. Never automated.
Hard overrides (bypass the score): a claim is forced out of the AUTO/ASSISTED path — regardless of STP score — when any of these is true: within the 2-year contestability window (Life), fraud risk ≥ 60, face/benefit above the auto-approval limit, beneficiary in dispute, or an OFAC hit on any party. These are policy, not ML — they are non-negotiable guardrails.

2 Fraud Risk Score

A separate weighted score, owned by the Fraud & Investigations context, computed by the Flink processor over windowed history.

Fraud = (velocity × 0.25)
       + (contestability_proximity × 0.20)
       + (beneficiary_anomaly × 0.20)
       + (documentation_inconsistency × 0.20)
       + (external_signal × 0.15)
FactorWeightSignal
velocity0.25Multiple claims on the same policy / claimant / beneficiary in a short window; rapid post-issue claim
contestability_proximity0.20Claim filed close to policy issue or just inside the contestability window (early-claim risk)
beneficiary_anomaly0.20Recent beneficiary change before loss; payee mismatch; bank account shared across unrelated claims
documentation_inconsistency0.20Dates / amounts / provider details that don't reconcile across documents; altered-document signals
external_signal0.15Industry data hit (e.g. MIB), watchlist, Death Master File mismatch on date of death, or known-fraud network link

Fraud tiers & actions

Fraud ScoreTierAction
< 30LOWNo action; fraud_inverse feeds STP normally
30 – 59ELEVATEDFlag for adjudicator attention; suppresses AUTO tier but allows ASSISTED with review
60 – 84HIGHfraud unit referral (SiuReferred); claim blocked from any automated path; payment hold applied
≥ 85CRITICALImmediate fraud unit + compliance notification; payment hard-held pending SiuCleared
Why some signals are counted more than once — on purpose. Contestability appears in three places: it lowers coverage_clear in the STP score, it is the contestability_proximity factor in the fraud score, and it is a hard guardrail that forces the claim out of the low-touch path. Fraud appears twice: the soft fraud_inverse factor and the hard gate at 60. The layering is intentional, and the two layers do different jobs — the soft factors shape queue routing among the non-gated claims, while the hard guardrails decide eligibility for automation at all. The gates do the decisive work (a claim at fraud 60 is forced out regardless of STP, so the soft fraud_inverse haircut is nearly inert by comparison); the soft couplings add resolution to where a borderline claim routes, not a second veto. We keep them because routing quality and automation-eligibility are genuinely separate concerns.

3 Rules First, ML Later

PhaseDecisioningFraudWhy
At launchDeterministic weighted rules (the formulas above), tuned with adjudicatorsRule-based signals + external data hitsExplainable from day one; regulators and adjudicators can audit every decision. No training data yet.
Once labeled history existsML propensity replaces the soft factors; hard guardrails stay as rulesSupervised fraud model trained on a meaningful stretch of labeled fraud unit outcomesEnough labeled history to train without noise. Guardrails never move to ML — they must stay auditable.
Design principle — never let ML decide a denial alone. ML can raise a claim out of the low-touch path (more scrutiny) but cannot be the sole basis of a denial. A denial always cites a coded reason mapped to a policy provision, decided by a human with authority. This keeps the platform defensible under UCSPA and state market-conduct exams.
Agent-assisted by design. Around these scores work first-class LLM agent workers that read documents, reconcile facts, and draft a decision packet with a cited rationale and confidence. They are the third leg of the decisioning evolution — deterministic rules, then ML on the soft factors, then agents reasoning on top — and the discipline holds at each leg: agents reason, humans decide. The auto-ticket lane stays rule-decided; humans review and approve on the assisted path; low-confidence or conflicting claims escalate to manual review. See AI-Assisted & Agentic Decisioning.
