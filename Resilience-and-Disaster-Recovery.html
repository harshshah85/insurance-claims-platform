Resilience & Disaster Recovery

Failure domains, recovery targets, and how an event-sourced platform that moves money survives the loss of a broker, a processor, or a whole region.

Prepared by Harsh Shah  |  June 2026
This is where event sourcing pays off. Because the log is the system of record and everything else is just a projection of it, most recovery is "replay from a durable log" rather than "restore a fragile database to an exact point and pray." This doc sets the recovery targets, the Kafka durability posture, and the part that actually matters: how in-flight work, including a half-finished payment, comes back without a mess.

1 Failure Domains & Recovery Targets

Recovery Time Objective (RTO) = how fast service is restored; Recovery Point Objective (RPO) = how much data, in time, can be lost. Set per failure domain, tightest where money and the record live.

FailureBlast radiusRTORPOHow it's met
Single broker / one availability zoneNone visible~00Replication factor 3 across 3 zones, min.insync.replicas=2; producers use acks=all. A lost broker is a non-event.
Stream processor crashOne processor lags brieflySeconds0Restart restores state from its compacted changelog and resumes from its committed offset. Exactly-once means no double effects.
Read-model store lossThat query view is stale/unavailableRebuild time0Read models are projections — drop and rebuild by replaying the log. No data is lost because none was the source of truth.
Poison event / bad deployOne consumer group stallsMinutes0Tiered retry → dead-letter queue isolates the bad message; roll back the deploy and replay. The log is untouched.
Whole-region lossPlatform failoverMinutes–hours= replication lag (bounded, > 0)Standby region fed by asynchronous cluster replication; failover promotes it. Because replication is async, the RPO is not zero — it is exactly the replication lag at the moment of loss: the most recent, not-yet-shipped events can be lost. §3 deals with what that means for a payment caught in that window.
Targets are tiered on purpose: the money-movement and audit paths get the tightest numbers; a stale dashboard can wait. Spending the same RTO budget everywhere would be over-engineering.

2 Kafka Durability Posture

ControlSettingWhy
Replication factor3, across 3 availability zonesSurvive a broker or a full zone with no data loss.

Regional-disaster protectionAsynchronous cluster replication to a standby regionThis is the only control that survives losing the whole primary region. Async keeps the hot path fast but means a non-zero RPO — the replication lag at the moment of loss (see §1).
Broker / zone durability + costTiered-storage segments in the same region's object storageThe log's older segments live in cheap, durable storage with RPO 0 — but only within the surviving region. This is a broker/zone-durability and fast-replay lever, not a regional-disaster backup; against a region loss it is gone with everything else unless that bucket is itself cross-region replicated.
Two mechanisms, two jobs — don't conflate them. The cross-region replica is your protection against losing a region, and it carries a real (> 0) RPO. Tiered storage is cost optimization plus broker/zone durability plus fast replay, with RPO 0 but only inside the region that survives. A region-loss event is covered by the first, not the second.

3 Recovering In-Flight Work — Including a Payment

The hard case: a region fails over while a disbursement is half-done. Because the saga's state is itself a stream of events, recovery is deterministic — and idempotency makes a replay safe.

Money movement runs as a saga whose every step is an event on payment.disbursement.v1 (scheduled → held → settled / failed). The saga has no hidden in-memory state to lose: on recovery the orchestrator rehydrates each in-flight payment by replaying its events and continues from exactly where it stopped — completing the next step or running the compensation. Commands carry idempotency keys (cmd.disburse), so even if a step is retried after failover, the downstream treasury/bank rail executes it once. A payment can never go out twice.

# A disbursement caught mid-flight by a regional failover before: PaymentScheduled ─► PaymentHeld (OFAC clear, dual-control approved) ─► … REGION LOST │ ▼ failover promotes standby region recover: orchestrator replays payment.disbursement.v1 for this payment_id │ last durable state = PaymentHeld ▼ resume: issue cmd.disburse (idempotency_key) ─► treasury executes ONCE ─► PaymentSettled │ (had a debit already happened pre-failover, the key makes the retry a no-op)
Why this is clean and not luck: the saga state lives in the log, not in a process; recovery is replay, not reconstruction; and idempotency keys make "did that step already happen?" a safe question to get wrong. The same three properties that make the platform auditable make it recoverable.

The edge case the async RPO forces: a lost PaymentSettled

Walk the one scenario where "the log is the truth" and "async RPO > 0" collide over real money. The primary region writes PaymentSettled — the bank rail did execute — but that event hasn't replicated when the region dies. The standby promotes, the saga rehydrates from the replicated log, sees the payment still at PaymentHeld, and re-issues cmd.disburse. The idempotency key protects the bank rail from a double-debit — but only if the key's dedupe state also survived the failover.

It does, because the dedupe state is derived from the same replicated payment.disbursement.v1 stream, not held in a side store with its own DR posture: on failover the orchestrator rebuilds the dedupe ledger by replaying that stream, so a cmd.disburse whose PaymentSettled did replicate is correctly suppressed. The genuinely exposed case is narrow — a settlement that executed at the bank but whose event sat in the unreplicated async window. There, replay can't see it, and the backstop is the one already run every day: daily treasury settlement-file reconciliation catches the re-disbursement (the bank shows two debits, or the second is rejected as a duplicate), and a compensating event corrects the log. The async window doesn't make double-payment impossible by replay alone — reconciliation is the named control that closes it.

4 Backups, Replay & Rebuild

  • The log is the backup. Long-retention topics on tiered storage mean a point-in-time rebuild is "replay from offset/timestamp," not a restore-and-pray.
  • Read models are disposable. Any projection can be dropped and rebuilt from the log; a corrupted view is fixed by redeploying the corrected projector and replaying, never by patching production rows.
  • Snapshots bound the cost. Aggregate snapshots (compacted changelogs) keep a full rebuild from taking forever — restore the snapshot, replay only events since.
  • Documents & cash live elsewhere. Large documents sit in the object/ECM store and the actual debit/credit in treasury; their own backup/DR posture is referenced, and Kafka carries only references and settled facts.
