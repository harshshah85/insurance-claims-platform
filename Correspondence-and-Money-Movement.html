Correspondence & Money Movement

Life & DI Claims Platform — Omnichannel Comms + the Disbursement Saga

Prepared by Harsh Shah  |  June 2026

Two integration surfaces, one principle. Both correspondence and money movement are event-driven and idempotent. Correspondence reacts to claim events and fans out across channels; money movement is a compensating saga that never uses a distributed transaction. Both are decoupled from the claim aggregate — they consume facts and report facts back, closing the loop.

1. Correspondence — Event-Driven & Omnichannel

Every inbound and outbound communication is an event on correspondence.v1. Three audiences: client, advisor, service rep.

Outbound (event → channel)

The Correspondence Trigger processor maps claim events to templated outbound messages, then the Correspondence Engine renders and delivers them according to the recipient's preference and consent.

Preference center & consent: the Correspondence Engine consults a per-party preference & consent model before sending — channel choice (email / SMS / secure message / print), language, accessibility, and legal opt-ins. A regulated notice (e.g. a denial) always has a compliant fallback channel (print/mail) even if the client opted out of digital.
Advisor & service-rep loop: advisors see their clients' claim correspondence in their desktop and can act (nudge a requirement, request a status). Service reps work from the same advisor_worklist read model. Both are first-class correspondence audiences, not afterthoughts — important for an advice-led distribution model.

2. Money Movement — the Disbursement Saga

A choreographed saga with compensation. Never a two-phase commit across the claims platform and treasury.

# Happy path ClaimApproved └─► PaymentScheduled # Payment aggregate created, idempotent payment_id └─► OFAC / sanctions screen ├─ hit ──► PaymentHeld (compliance) ──► manual clear / cancel └─ clear ─► tax determination (1099-R / withholding) └─► dual-control approval # two distinct approvers for > threshold └─► cmd.disburse.v1 ──► Treasury / Disbursement platform ├─ success ─► PaymentSettled ──► correspondence + ledger └─ fail ────► PaymentFailed ──► retry / compensate # Compensation (e.g. settled then reversed — wrong payee) PaymentSettled ──► reversal command ──► PaymentReversed (new event, never a delete) ──► treasury recoup
ControlHow it worksWhy
Idempotent payment_idA payment_id settles at most once; a duplicate cmd.disburse is a no-opA retried command must never double-pay a beneficiary
OFAC / sanctions screenEvery payee screened before funds move; hit → PaymentHeldLegal requirement; blocks prohibited payments
Dual controlPayments above a threshold require two distinct human approversSegregation of duties; SOX-style control over disbursement
Tax determinationWithholding & 1099-R basis computed before settlement (DI benefits taxability depends on premium funding)IRS reporting accuracy; avoids downstream corrections
Payment holdFraud ≥ 60, beneficiary dispute, or recert overdue holds the disbursementCouples decisioning/fraud guardrails to the cash gate
Reconciliationpayment_ledger reconciled daily against treasury settlement fileKafka carries the command + settled fact; treasury is the cash ledger of record
Why a saga, not 2PC: the claims platform and the treasury/disbursement system are independent services with independent failure modes. A distributed transaction would couple their availability and still not span the external bank rail. A saga makes every step an explicit, observable state with a compensating action — which is also exactly what an auditor wants to see. (Rationale recorded as an ADR in Event-Driven Decision Records.)

3. DI Recurring Disbursement

Life pays once; DI pays a recurring benefit. The same saga runs each period, gated by recertification.

BenefitPeriodOpened ──► schedule recurring PaymentScheduled (per period) │ ├─ RecertificationReceived ──► release period payment ──► saga ──► PaymentSettled ├─ RecertificationDue & overdue ──► PaymentHeld (suppress until received) └─ BenefitOffsetApplied (e.g. SSDI) ──► reduce period amount before disbursement

Closed loop: delivery confirmations and settlement facts flow back as events (CorrespondenceDelivered, PaymentSettled), updating the client-facing read model and the regulatory timers. The client portal shows "payment sent" because a real settlement event arrived — not because a button was clicked.
