Claims Onboarding Workflow

Life & DI Claims Platform — New Product & New Claim Intake Onboarding

Prepared by Harsh Shah  |  June 2026

Two kinds of onboarding. Scenario A — onboarding a new product / plan (a new Life or DI product that can now be claimed against): a one-time engineering/config exercise. Scenario B — onboarding a new claim at intake (FNOL): the high-volume, per-claim runtime flow. They share the platform but differ entirely in cadence and who does the work.

1. Scenario A — New Product / Plan Onboarding

One-time. Performed by product + platform teams when a new Life/DI product becomes claimable.

Step 1 — Map coverage to claim rules [semi-auto]
Define which loss events the product covers, exclusions, elimination period (DI), face/benefit structure, and contestability terms. Encode as decisioning rules consumed by STP scoring.
Step 2 — Register / extend event schemas [manual + CI gate]
If the product needs new fields, evolve the relevant Avro schemas (additive only) and pass the Schema Registry compatibility check in CI. New product variants almost always fit existing v1 schemas.
Step 3 — Confirm Policy Admin CDC coverage [auto]
Verify the new product's policies flow through Kafka Connect into policy.snapshot.v1 with in-force/coverage/contestability fields populated. No new pipeline — the CDC connector already covers the source.
Step 4 — Define requirement templates [manual]
Which documents/forms this product requires at claim (e.g. DI adds attending-physician statement & occupation details). Drives RequirementRaised events.
Step 5 — Correspondence templates & regulatory timers [manual]
Acknowledgement, requirements, decision, and payment templates per product; wire the statutory SLA timers (acknowledge/decide/pay) for the product's jurisdictions.
Step 6 — Money-movement & tax config [semi-auto]
Payment methods, withholding/1099-R treatment (Life death benefit typically non-taxable; DI taxability depends on premium funding), auto-approval limit for STP.
Step 7 — Shadow & go-live [manual]
Run new-product claims in shadow (decisions computed, not actioned) to validate STP scoring, then enable. First live claims watched closely.
As the platform matures, most of Scenario A becomes configuration, not code: product rules, requirement templates, and timers are data. Adding a product becomes a config change validated in shadow rather than a release.

2. Scenario B — New Claim Intake (FNOL)

Per-claim, high-volume, mostly automated. This is the runtime onboarding of a claim into the platform.

Channel (client / advisor / rep / IVR / mail) ─► Intake API │ [auto] capture FNOL, attach channel, emit FnolCaptured (via outbox) ▼ Policy Resolution ├─ resolved ──► PolicyResolved + CoverageConfirmed + ContestabilityFlagged? [auto] └─ unresolved ► PolicyUnresolved → QUARANTINE [auto-detect, manual recovery] ▼ Party & Identity ─► ClaimantRegistered, BeneficiaryIdentified, IdentityVerified [auto IDV; manual if challenged] ▼ Requirements ─► RequirementRaised (per product template) [auto] ▼ Scoring ─► StpEligibilityScored + FraudScored [auto] ▼ ├─ STP ≥ 85 & fraud < 60 & non-contestable ─► AUTO / low-touch ticket └─ else ─► human queue (assisted / standard / SIU)

3. Quarantine Recovery (Unresolved Policy)

An FNOL whose policy number can't be resolved against policy.snapshot.v1 is quarantined, never dropped — the claimant has already been told their claim is received, so losing it is unacceptable.

PolicyUnresolved ─► quarantine read model (claim is live, status "in research") ├─ [auto] re-attempt match on each policy.snapshot.v1 update (late CDC, fuzzy match on name/DOB) ├─ [auto] escalate to service-rep worklist after N hours with SLA timer running └─ [resolved] ─► PolicyResolved ─► claim rejoins the normal flow at Party & Identity
Why this matters: in Life claims the most common quarantine cause is a beneficiary who has the policy number slightly wrong or only knows the insured's name. The recovery flow keeps the regulatory acknowledgement clock honest while research happens in the background — the claim is never silently stuck.
