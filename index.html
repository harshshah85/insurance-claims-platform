Reference Architecture

Event-Driven, AI-Assisted
Claims Platform

A reference architecture for processing insurance claims as a stream of events on Kafka — event sourcing through review, fraud checks, correspondence, and payment, with a low-touch "auto-ticket" path for the clean, simple ones. On top sits an agentic, human-in-the-loop decisioning layer: language-model agents read documents, reconcile facts, and draft a recommended decision for a person to approve — agents reason, people decide. Demonstrated end-to-end on Life & Disability claims (a death claim is filed by a beneficiary; a disability-income claim by the policyholder).

Key Design Decisions

Decision 01

Store the claim as a list of events

We keep each claim as the ordered list of things that happened to it, instead of a row we overwrite. Screens read from simple copies built from that list. The payoff: a complete, tamper-evident history for free — exactly what regulators and disputes need.

Decision 02

The policy system stays the source of truth

We don't copy or own policy data. The existing policy admin system stays the authority on what's in force, what's covered, and the key dates; we just read a live feed of its changes. If a filed claim can't be matched to a policy, we hold it for research rather than drop it.

Decision 03

Auto-ticket the clean claims

Clean, low-risk claims are decided automatically and auto-ticketed — no queue, no human. A simple score (we call it the STP score) sorts them: ≥ 85 fully automatic, 65–84 a quick human sign-off, 40–64 full review, < 40 complex / fraud unit. Plain rules first, machine learning later.

Decision 04

Apps read through one stable set of APIs

The client portal, advisor desktop, and service-rep tools never touch the raw event stream. They go through one versioned set of claims APIs, so we can change how things work inside without breaking the screens.

Decision 05

STP & Fraud Scoring Formulas

STP = (policy_in_force × 0.25) + (coverage_clear × 0.20) + (doc_completeness × 0.20) + (identity_verified × 0.15) + (fraud_inverse × 0.10) + (amount_in_band × 0.10). Fraud risk is a separate weighted score; ≥ 60 forces fraud unit referral and blocks the low-touch path.

Decision 06

Pay out in small, reversible steps

Paying out crosses our platform, the treasury system, and a bank — so we never wrap it in one big transaction. Each step is its own tracked stage that can be undone if a later one fails. Two approvers for large payments, sanctions screening, holds, and tax handling are all explicit steps — and a payment can never go out twice.

Decision 07

Agents reason, people decide

LLM agent workers — first-class processors — read documents, reconcile facts, and draft a recommended decision with cited rationale and confidence. But a human is the decision-maker on every AI-touched claim, no denial is ever model-only, and autonomy on clean claims is an earned, governed step. The agents reason on a derived claims.ai.* stream; people keep decision authority.
