Event-Driven Decision Records

Life & DI Claims Platform — Architecture Decision Reference

Prepared by Harsh Shah  |  June 2026

Why ADRs, not a vendor bake-off. The defining choices on this platform are architectural patterns, not product logos. Each record below states the decision, the options weighed, the trade-off, and the consequence. Decisions are deliberately reversible where cheap and deliberately locked where a reversal would be catastrophic (money movement, audit).

ADR-01 — Event Sourcing vs. CRUD as system of record ACCEPTED

Context: claims require a complete, ordered, tamper-evident history for regulators and disputed decisions; state is naturally a sequence of events (notified, requirement satisfied, decided, paid).

OptionProsCons
Event sourcingBuilt-in audit trail; time-travel; rebuildable read models; natural fit for the domainSteeper model; eventual consistency on the read side; replay discipline needed
CRUD + audit tableFamiliar; simple readsAudit is bolted-on and can drift from truth; no causal history; harder market-conduct story
Decision: Event sourcing for the Claim aggregate; CRUD-style read models as derived projections. The log is the truth; rows are disposable.

ADR-02 — Choreography vs. Orchestration ACCEPTED

Context: the claim journey crosses 9 bounded contexts; money movement and recertification have strict ordering and compensation needs.

OptionProsCons
Choreography (default)Loose coupling; contexts evolve independently; no central bottleneckHarder to see the end-to-end flow; emergent behavior
Orchestration (where needed)Explicit control flow; easy to reason about a sagaCentral coupling; the orchestrator becomes a critical dependency
Decision: Choreography-first across contexts; explicit orchestration only for the money-movement saga, where ordered compensation and dual control justify a coordinator. Mixed by intent, not by accident.

ADR-03 — Saga vs. Two-Phase Commit for money movement ACCEPTED

Context: disbursement spans the claims platform, treasury, and an external bank rail — systems with independent availability that no single transaction can span.

OptionProsCons
Saga + compensationNo distributed lock; each step observable; compensations are auditable; resilient to partial failureEventual consistency; must design compensations (e.g. reversal events)
2PC / XAACID across participantsCouples availability; doesn't span the bank rail; poor failure isolation
Decision: Saga with idempotent commands and compensating events. Reversals are new events, never deletes. (Detailed in Correspondence & Money Movement.)

ADR-04 — A durable log (Kafka) vs. a managed streaming service vs. an event router ACCEPTED

Context: we need a durable, replayable, ordered log that is the system of record — not just a way to move messages around — with a mature stream-processing and connector ecosystem.

CapabilityDurable log (Kafka)Managed streaming serviceEvent router / bus
Long retention as the recordYes (compaction + tiered storage)Limited retentionNo — it routes, it doesn't store
Ordered, keyed partitionsYesYes (sharded)No ordering guarantee
Replay from any pointYesOnly within retentionNo
Stream processing & joinsMature (Kafka Streams + Flink)Library-basedN/A
Schema registry & connectorsRich ecosystemSmallerLimited connectors
Decision: Kafka — the only option that is a true replayable log of record with the processing and connector ecosystem the platform leans on. A managed event router can still handle simple fan-out at the edges.

ADR-05 — Avro vs. Protobuf vs. JSON Schema ACCEPTED

Context: 36 event types consumed by many teams over years; schema evolution must be governed and safe.

OptionProsCons
Avro + Schema RegistryCompact; first-class registry support; strong, enforced compatibility modes; great fit for KafkaLess human-readable on the wire
ProtobufCompact; cross-language; good toolingCompatibility rules less batteries-included with the registry workflow
JSON SchemaHuman-readable; easy onboardingVerbose; weaker compactness; easier to drift
Decision: Avro with registry-enforced BACKWARD_TRANSITIVE on event topics. Compatibility is enforced in CI — schema breakage cannot reach production.

ADR-06 — Rules-based vs. ML decisioning at launch ACCEPTED

Context: no labeled history at launch; regulators require explainable decisions; denials must be defensible.

OptionProsCons
Rules first, ML once labeled history existsExplainable day one; auditable; no training data needed; guardrails stay rules foreverLess adaptive early; manual tuning
ML from launchPotentially higher automationNo clean labels yet → noisy; hard to defend a denial; erodes trust
Decision: Deterministic weighted scoring (STP + fraud) first; supervised ML only once there are enough labeled outcomes to train on. Hard guardrails (contestability, OFAC, limits) never move to ML. (See Claims Decisioning & Fraud.)

How these connect: ADR-01 (event sourcing) makes ADR-04 (Kafka as log of record) almost forced. ADR-02/03 (choreography + saga) follow from having an event log. ADR-05 keeps it safe over time. ADR-06 keeps it defensible to regulators. The decisions reinforce each other — which is the point of recording them together.
