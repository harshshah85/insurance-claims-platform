Event-Driven Decision Records

The honest "why" behind the platform's biggest choices — and what each one cost. A short decision record for every call that shaped the architecture, written so a future reader can disagree on purpose, not by accident.

Prepared by Harsh Shah  |  June 2026
Why ADRs, and not a vendor bake-off. The choices that define this platform are architectural patterns, not product logos. Each record below gives the decision, the options I weighed, the trade-off, and what it commits us to. Some are kept easy to reverse; others are locked down hard, because reversing them — money movement, audit — would be catastrophic.

ADR-01 — Event Sourcing vs. CRUD as system of record ACCEPTED

Context: claims require a complete, ordered, tamper-evident history for regulators and disputed decisions; state is naturally a sequence of events (notified, requirement satisfied, decided, paid).

OptionProsCons
Event sourcingBuilt-in audit trail; time-travel; rebuildable read models; natural fit for the domainSteeper model; eventual consistency on the read side; replay discipline needed
CRUD + audit tableFamiliar; simple readsAudit is bolted-on and can drift from truth; no causal history; harder market-conduct story
Decision: Event sourcing for the Claim aggregate; CRUD-style read models as derived projections. The log is the truth; rows are disposable.

ADR-02 — Choreography vs. Orchestration ACCEPTED

Context: the claim journey crosses 9 bounded contexts; money movement and recertification have strict ordering and compensation needs.

OptionProsCons
Choreography (default)Loose coupling; contexts evolve independently; no central bottleneckHarder to see the end-to-end flow; emergent behavior
Orchestration (where needed)Explicit control flow; easy to reason about a sagaCentral coupling; the orchestrator becomes a critical dependency
Decision: Choreography-first across contexts; explicit orchestration only for the money-movement saga, where ordered compensation and dual control justify a coordinator. Mixed by intent, not by accident.

ADR-03 — Saga vs. Two-Phase Commit for money movement ACCEPTED

Context: disbursement spans the claims platform, treasury, and an external bank rail — systems with independent availability that no single transaction can span.

OptionProsCons
Saga + compensationNo distributed lock; each step observable; compensations are auditable; resilient to partial failureEventual consistency; must design compensations (e.g. reversal events)
2PC / XAACID across participantsCouples availability; doesn't span the bank rail; poor failure isolation
Decision: Saga with idempotent commands and compensating events. Reversals are new events, never deletes. (Detailed in Correspondence & Money Movement.)

ADR-04 — A durable log (Kafka) vs. a managed streaming service vs. an event router ACCEPTED

Context: we need a durable, replayable, ordered log that is the system of record — not just a way to move messages around — with a mature stream-processing and connector ecosystem.

CapabilityDurable log (Kafka)Managed streaming serviceEvent router / bus
Long retention as the recordYes (long retention + tiered storage)Limited retentionNo — it routes, it doesn't store
Ordered, keyed partitionsYesYes (sharded)No ordering guarantee
Replay from any pointYesOnly within retentionNo
Stream processing & joinsMature (Kafka Streams + Flink)Library-basedN/A
Schema registry & connectorsRich ecosystemSmallerLimited connectors
Decision: Kafka — the only option that is a true replayable log of record with the processing and connector ecosystem the platform leans on. A managed event router can still handle simple fan-out at the edges.

ADR-05 — Avro vs. Protobuf vs. JSON Schema ACCEPTED

Context: 36 event types consumed by many teams over years; schema evolution must be governed and safe.

OptionProsCons
Avro + Schema RegistryCompact; first-class registry support; strong, enforced compatibility modes; great fit for KafkaLess human-readable on the wire
ProtobufCompact; cross-language; good toolingCompatibility rules less batteries-included with the registry workflow
JSON SchemaHuman-readable; easy onboardingVerbose; weaker compactness; easier to drift
Decision: Avro with registry-enforced BACKWARD_TRANSITIVE on event topics. Compatibility is enforced in CI — schema breakage cannot reach production.

ADR-06 — Rules-based vs. ML decisioning at launch ACCEPTED

Context: no labeled history at launch; regulators require explainable decisions; denials must be defensible.

OptionProsCons
Rules first, ML once labeled history existsExplainable day one; auditable; no training data needed; guardrails stay rules foreverLess adaptive early; manual tuning
ML from launchPotentially higher automationNo clean labels yet → noisy; hard to defend a denial; erodes trust
Decision: Deterministic weighted scoring (STP + fraud) first; supervised ML only once there are enough labeled outcomes to train on. Hard guardrails (contestability, OFAC, limits) never move to ML. (See Claims Decisioning & Fraud.)

ADR-07 — LLM/agent assist as advisory vs. autonomous AI decisioning ACCEPTED

Context: an LLM/agent layer can read documents, reconcile facts, and draft a decision far faster than a human. The question is how much authority it holds — does it decide claims, or recommend to a human who decides?

OptionProsCons
Advisory now → governed graduation to decidingHuman stays accountable; every recommendation auditable; defensible under AI-governance rules; builds an evidence base (acceptance/accuracy) before any autonomySlower to full automation; humans still in the assisted loop
Autonomous AI decisioning from day oneHighest automation immediatelyNo accountable decision-maker; hard to defend a denial; unproven model deciding money movement; regulatory exposure
Decision: The LLM produces analysis + a recommended decision with cited detail; a human is the decision-maker on every AI-touched claim, and the deterministic auto-ticket lane stays rule-decided. A claim class moves from "recommend" to "AI decides" only once the model has proven out against human reviewers — a deliberate, governed, one-directional step. Hard guardrails and the human-authored-denial rule hold at every stage. (Extends ADR-06; see AI-Assisted & Agentic Decisioning.)

ADR-08 — Kafka as the system of record vs. a database of record ACCEPTED

Context: ADR-01 chose event sourcing and ADR-04 chose Kafka as the log technology — but one prior question is load-bearing and easy to leave implicit: is the durable system of record the Kafka log itself, or a database, with Kafka only the streaming backbone in front of it? The whole platform leans on the first answer ("the log is the truth"), so the trade-off deserves to be written down, not backed into.

DimensionKafka as system of recordDatabase of record + Kafka backbone
Ordering & historyNative — partition order is claim order; replay is first-classModeled with sequence/version columns; replay means querying then re-publishing
Audit storyThe log is the audit record directlyAudit is a query over an events table — fine, but one more layer
Durability vs. total cluster lossNeeds an explicit off-cluster path (async replica + object storage)Mature DB backup / point-in-time-restore tooling
Operational dependencyWrites block if Kafka is unhealthyDB write succeeds independently; Kafka can lag or restart
Two-systems-in-sync burdenNone — one source of truthReal — the outbox now sits on the critical write path, not just the edges
Real-time reaction (fraud, STP, agents)Native — everything is already a streamA CDC hop before processors can react
Regulator / auditor familiarityLower (a streaming log)Higher (SQL)
Decision: Kafka is the system of record. For a platform whose dominant workload is many independent processors reacting to claim events in real time — fraud scoring, STP, projectors, agents — the streaming-native shape fits far better than a database-first design, and it removes the two-systems-in-sync burden from the hot path. But the choice buys an obligation: the off-cluster durability story is no longer optional. The cross-region replica and object-storage backup in Resilience & Disaster Recovery are not a nice-to-have — they are the other half of this decision.

How these connect: ADR-01 (event sourcing) makes ADR-04 (Kafka as log of record) almost forced. ADR-02/03 (choreography + saga) follow from having an event log. ADR-05 keeps it safe over time. ADR-06 keeps it defensible to regulators, and ADR-07 extends that same discipline to the LLM/agent layer — AI advises, humans decide, autonomy is earned. ADR-08 names the choice that ADR-01 and ADR-04 quietly assume — Kafka is the record — and makes the off-cluster durability posture in Resilience & Disaster Recovery the explicit other half of it. The decisions reinforce each other — which is the point of recording them together.
