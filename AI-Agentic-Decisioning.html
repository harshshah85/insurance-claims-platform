AI-Assisted & Agentic Decisioning

Event-Driven, AI-Assisted Claims Platform — Where AI Helps, Where a Person Decides, and Where It Hands Off

Prepared by Harsh Shah  |  June 2026
Here's the deal. Every claim still runs the full check pipeline — all the deterministic policy, coverage, identity, fraud, and compliance checks, with the same complete event trail. The AI skips none of it. What it does is the judgment-shaped work that eats an adjudicator's day: reading documents, reconciling facts, drafting a decision with a cited rationale, writing the letters. And what it hands back is a proposal, not a decision. A person reviews and approves on the assisted path, the clean auto-ticket path stays rule-decided, and anything the AI can't ground or isn't sure about goes to a human.

1 A Third Decision Layer — Not a Replacement

The AI sits on top of the existing two-score model. It feeds and explains the decision; it never becomes the guardrail.

LayerWho runs itWhat it doesCan it be wrong & recover?
GuardrailsDeterministic rules (code)Non-negotiable gates — contestability window, fraud ≥ 60, OFAC hit, amount over the auto-approval limit, beneficiary dispute. Pure policy.Never delegated to a model. Same code as today.
ScoringRules → ML (existing)The STP eligibility score and the fraud risk score already documented in Claims Decisioning & Fraud.Explainable; auditable per factor.
Reasoning newLLM agents + skillsExtract from documents, reconcile across them, assemble the STP factors into a decision packet with a cited rationale and a confidence, draft correspondence.Cites its sources, reports confidence, and can abstain → escalate.
Today: the LLM recommends, a human decides. The LLM's job right now is to produce a full analysis and a recommended decision, with the supporting detail — extracted facts, cited evidence, the per-factor reasoning, and a confidence — packaged for a human to consume and act on. A person is the decision-maker on every claim the model's judgment touches. The rule-based auto-ticket lane (deterministic STP + guardrails, no LLM) stays as it is; the model's judgment does not auto-approve or auto-deny while it is still maturing.

Maturity path — from recommend to decide

StageWhat the LLM doesWho decides
NowAnalyzes the claim and recommends a decision with full detail for a human to reviewHuman — the decision-maker on every AI-touched claim
As the model proves outSame analysis, measured against human decisions; acceptance and accuracy trackedHuman, with confidence and override metrics building the evidence base
As AI maturesDirectly processes and decides the clean, well-grounded claims within the guardrailsAI auto-approves / denies the clean ones; humans handle the exceptions
The progression is deliberate and one-directional: we only let the model decide a class of claims once its recommendations have been right, at volume, against human reviewers — and the hard guardrails (contestability, fraud, OFAC, amount limits) and the human-authored-denial rule stay in force at every stage. Moving a claim class from "recommend" to "decide" is an explicit, governed step, not a default.

2 Agent Topology

A supervisor orchestrator per claim, fanning out to single-responsibility specialist agents that mirror the bounded contexts. Every agent subscribes to events and emits events — so every AI action lands in the log and is auditable forever.

How this fits the event backbone. The agent workers are first-class processors that reason off the committed log and draft onto a dedicated claims.ai.* stream — derived the way CQRS read models are derived. By design they speed up the existing nine contexts rather than forming a tenth, and authority stays with humans: the core decision, fraud, and money-movement events remain owned by their contexts and are produced by a human or the deterministic auto-ticket lane. Agents reason, humans decide.

3 Skills & Tools

Split the way the agent world does. A tool is a deterministic integration with no judgment. A skill is a reusable, model-driven procedure. Money-moving and state-changing tools always sit behind a human gate or a guardrail.

The dividing rule. Skills produce drafts and findings. Tools that move money or change claim state are reached only through a human gate or a deterministic guardrail. An agent can read freely; it cannot act on the world on its own.

4 Human-in-the-Loop Gates

Mapped onto the four decision tiers that already exist. The further down the tiers, the more the human leads and the AI only assists.

TierWhat the AI doesThe human gate
AUTO  ≥ 85Prepares the full packet; rules + scores make the auto-ticket call inside the guardrailsNo per-claim human. But: a confidence floor is required, the AI can never deny, and a sampled share is pulled for human QA to catch drift
ASSISTED  65–84Delivers a complete decision packet — recommendation, evidence, citations, confidenceOne-click confirm or correct. This is the core "review the AI's findings, then approve" loop
STANDARD  40–64Copilot — extracts, summarizes, surfaces the facts and the score breakdownHuman adjudicates fully; the AI is assist-only
COMPLEX / fraud unit  < 40Assembles the narrative and the evidence packSenior adjudicator / investigator decide; the AI never automates any part
Any denial — the AI may draft the coded reason and rationale, but a human with authority always makes the call. ML can raise a claim out of the low-touch path; it can never be the sole basis of a denial.   Money movement — the AI proposes; a human approves above threshold and the disbursement saga is unchanged (dual control stays).

5 When the AI Hands Off

Escalation is a normal, expected outcome — not a failure. The agent emits EscalatedToManualReview with a reason, and the claim joins the right human worklist.

TriggerWhy it escalates
Confidence below threshold on any extraction or decision factorAn unsure model output is worth less than an honest "send this to a human"
Evidence conflicts / reconciliation failsDates, amounts, or provider details don't agree across documents
A hard guardrail tripsContestability, fraud ≥ 60, OFAC, amount over limit, beneficiary dispute — policy, not model
Document quality too poor to extract reliablyBetter to ask the claimant again than to guess
Out-of-distribution / novel claim patternThe model has no business auto-handling something it hasn't seen
A required tool or data source is unavailableCan't verify in-force → can't responsibly proceed
Decisioning and Fraud agents disagreeConflict between agents is a signal for a human, not a tie to break automatically
Abstention is a first-class output. "I don't know — route this to a human" is an encouraged, well-rewarded agent result, not an error. The platform is tuned so that the cost of a wrong auto-decision is always higher than the cost of one more manual review.

6 Governance & Guardrails for the AI Itself

The part a regulator or a market-conduct examiner will ask about. Each of these is a non-negotiable property of the reasoning layer.

GuardrailWhat it means in practice
GroundingEvery finding cites the source document and field it came from. No free-floating assertions — if it can't be cited, it isn't asserted.
Determinism stays in codeThe hard guardrails and the scoring math are never delegated to the model. The AI can recommend; it cannot redefine policy.
Full audit metadataEvery AI event records model version, the inputs it saw, its output, and its confidence — the same forever-auditable trail the scores already get.
No model-only denialA denial always cites a coded reason mapped to a policy provision and is signed by a human with authority. Keeps the platform defensible under UCSPA and state market-conduct exams.
Evaluation + sampled QAAn offline evaluation harness plus a sampled human QA on the AUTO lane watches for drift; false-auto rate stays the number we never let creep.

7 Evaluation & the Feedback Flywheel

What makes the reasoning layer get better over time — and the governance that keeps "better" from quietly meaning "more autonomous."

Every human decision is also a label. When an adjudicator confirms or corrects an ASSISTED packet, or decides a STANDARD claim outright, that outcome lands on claims.decision.v1 next to the agent's recommendation — a clean, growing set of model-said / human-did pairs. That set is the flywheel: it trains the next fraud and auto-ticket models and scores the next version of the agents.

# The feedback flywheel human decision (confirm · correct · decide) ──► claims.decision.v1 (paired with the agent's claims.ai.v1 recommendation) │ ▼ labeled model-said / human-did outcomes training & evaluation sets ──► candidate model / prompt version │ ▼ offline eval on a golden set (accuracy vs human · grounding · abstention precision · false-auto rate) │ must clear thresholds to ship ▼ shadow ──► canary ──► full (online: grounding rate · packet acceptance · abstention precision · p95) │ ▼ better recommendations next time — NOT more autonomy (that is a separate, governed step)

Offline evaluation — earn the right to ship

A curated golden set of historical claims with known-correct outcomes is the gate. Every candidate model or prompt version is scored against it before it can reach production — accuracy versus the human decision, grounding rate (every finding cited), abstention precision and recall (does it decline the right ones), and the false-auto rate that the AUTO lane never lets creep. Prompts and models are versioned artifacts, and the version is already stamped on every claims.ai.v1 event, so any recommendation is reproducible.

Online — watch, roll out, roll back

A passing version rolls out shadow → canary → full, watched on the same metrics surfaced in Observability (grounding rate, packet-acceptance rate, abstention precision, agent p95). Any regression rolls back instantly — a config flip, because the model is a swappable dependency, not baked in.

The flywheel feeds quality, not authority. More labels make the recommendations sharper; they do not let the model start deciding on its own. Today a human is the decision-maker on every AI-touched claim. Moving a claim class from "recommend" to "decide" is a deliberate, governed, one-directional step — evidence from this very flywheel is what justifies it, but the promotion is a human sign-off, never an automatic consequence of a good evaluation. (See ADR-06 and the maturity path in §1.)
